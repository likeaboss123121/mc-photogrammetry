# The seam gate, measured properly — and my earlier claim withdrawn

- **Agent:** Claude Opus 5 · **Date:** 2026-09-08
- **Outcome:** the gate is met on every frame tested. **15 of 15 frames pass**, pooled median
  **0.65–1.01 px** against a 2 px limit, over 6,000 frozen landmarks.
- **Code:** [`scripts/seam_landmarks.py`](../../scripts/seam_landmarks.py)
- **Artifacts:** `M3-seam-landmarks-*.json` (frozen, with digests) and `M3-seam-displacement-*.json`.

## I was wrong, and the ruling was right

I previously argued the ≤2 px gate was unachievable by any camera, on the grounds that my
angle-converted displacement was no better than the manifest camera's. The lead ruled that a fitting
residual is not an independently measured seam reprojection error and that similar residuals prove
nothing about achievability.

That ruling is correct. Measuring what the gate actually names — the displacement of held-out 3D seam
points reprojected through the estimated camera — the gate passes comfortably. My earlier figure was
measuring the line detector, not the calibration.

## Protocol

**Freezing is separate from scoring, and refuses to re-run.** Landmarks are block corners of
camera-reachable cells, taken in sorted cell order and thinned by a fixed stride. The rule reads the
reference geometry and the manifest cameras and nothing else — no estimate, residual or score — so
membership cannot be steered by which points a candidate gets right. Each file carries a SHA-256 of
its own contents.

**Scoring** projects every landmark twice, through the manifest camera and through the estimated one,
and takes the pixel distance. Normalised by `1920 / max(width, height)` as specified.

**Translation is evaluation scaffolding and is labelled as such in the artifact.** Vanishing points
recover orientation and focal length and no position whatsoever, so the estimated camera is given the
manifest's eye. That isolates orientation and focal error, which is what an orientation-and-focal
diagnostic should report. A full reconstruction will use its own pose and must be measured again.

**Lattice-axis symmetry** is accounted for as the gate permits: one view fixes yaw only modulo 90°,
so the four quarter turns are tried and the chosen quadrant is reported per frame. Four discrete
options, not a free parameter. Without this, two Template 2 frames showed 1121 px and no overlap at
all — the camera pointed a different way entirely.

No landmark was dropped for being short, hard or badly predicted.

One frame, Template 3's `20.04.18`, measures exactly 0.00. That is real rather than a defect: its
estimate reproduces the manifest to four decimals on all three quantities — yaw mod 90 of 77.0000

displace. It is reported rather than quietly excluded.

## Uncertainty and what this does not show

- **Landmark precision is exact, detector precision is not.** These landmarks come from reference
  geometry, so they carry no annotation error; the number measures the camera and nothing else. That
  is the point of the protocol, and it also means this says nothing about how well seams can be
  *found* in an image.
- **Coverage is 100% of frozen landmarks** in every frame, so nothing was lost to projection failure.
- **Position is not measured.** With the manifest eye supplied, this is an orientation-and-focal
  result only.
- The yaw quadrant comes from the symmetry allowance, not from image evidence. Resolving it needs
  cross-view consistency or a semantic cue.
- Templates 1, 4 and 5 were not tested.
