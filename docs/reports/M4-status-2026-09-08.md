## What is solid

**Plane distance, from a stated length.** `scale_from_known_length` places a
plane to 0.1-0.4% from one image, given two user-named points and a block
count. Needs no manifest, no second view, no feature matching.

**Cross-view translation registration, from stated plane identity and stated
quadrant.** `register_axis` recovers all three Template 2 cameras on all three
axes to within 0.004 blocks of the manifest (read only to score), given
`SharedPlaneLink` (a person's assertion that two views see the same surface)
and, for the two horizontal axes, `--quadrant` (a person's assertion of which
quarter-turn aligns one view's axes with another's). A wrong link cannot be
caught internally - proven algebraically, not just observed - so this is
honest about what it does and does not validate.

Both of these needed a **stated fact** instead of an automatically-derived one,
and that is not incidental. It is the same shape as the standing project
principle that camera manifests are scaffolding, not architecture: a real user
will not have F3 data, but a real user *can* look at two photographs and say
"that's the same floor" or "rotate this one a quarter-turn" - cheap, and immune
to the repetition that defeats appearance matching.

## What is a genuine wall, not a tuning problem

**Per-cell occupancy from image appearance.** Five cues tried:

| cue | result |
|---|---|
| planar region proposal | decided 5-13% of tiles |
| period strength | did not identify a plane normal at all |
| global phase agreement | chance (measured on noise) |
| local-window phase agreement | **AUC 0.523** against real ground truth |
| brightness-consensus | **AUC 0.502** against real ground truth |

The last two carry a proper measurement, built for this: for every judged
cell, reconstruct its world location from the real camera pose (read only to
evaluate) and check whether a genuine, camera-reachable reference block face
sits there, then compute AUC between truth-positive and truth-negative cell
scores. 0.5 is no separation at all. Neither cue reaches it.

One promising-looking result (a single hand-picked window scoring 0.466
against a measured chance ceiling of 0.409) did not hold up when measured
broadly - which is exactly why that broad measurement was built before
touching anything downstream of it. See the two addenda in the supported-plane
report for the full arc, including the correction of my own initial optimism.

**Caveat on scope:** the ground-truth measurement is concentrated on one view
(13.24.33, 168 of 169 judged cells), viewed at an unusually oblique, elevated
angle. I would not generalize "appearance cues cannot work on Minecraft
interiors" from this alone - only that none of the five tried have worked on
the evidence gathered so far. A more head-on view or a different fixture might
tell a different story, untested.

## What looks unrecoverable in principle, not just untried

**Horizontal-axis quadrant, from images alone.** `axis_directions` recovers
yaw only modulo 90 degrees from a single view - a structural symmetry of
Manhattan-world calibration, not a measurement limitation. Breaking that
symmetry needs either a second independent signal (relative pose via feature
correspondence - already tried via PyCOLMAP, 0/42 usable pairs, defeated by
the same repeated textures) or a stated fact. I do not see a third option, and
did not find one.

**Real joint multi-view refinement.** Checked before building it, not after:
with one observation per (view, axis), any path between two views - however
long - reduces to the exact same relative position, because intermediate
values cancel out of the algebra completely, regardless of their own noise
(verified with a deliberately corrupted intermediate value). There is no
redundant, independently-noisy path in this data model for a refinement step
to average over. Building one would be infrastructure that provably does
nothing; retracted in comms before shipping it.

## The shape of what M4 has actually found

Three separate mechanisms - length, plane identity, quadrant - all needed a
stated fact rather than an automatic derivation, and in each case the reason
is the same: Minecraft's repeated textures defeat correspondence-based
methods, and a single or even multiple Manhattan-world views carry a real
information gap that nothing image-only closes. That is not a series of
unrelated failures; it is the same wall found three times. Per-cell occupancy
looks like a fourth instance of it, though less certainly - it is an empirical
finding (measured, not proven impossible) rather than a structural one.

## Left undone, deliberately

- Per-cell occupancy: unsolved. Not currently being pursued further without a
  specific new idea - five attempts is enough to stop guessing.
- Automatic quadrant resolution: not pursued - looks structurally
  unrecoverable from images alone.
- Export-cell / bounds construction from registered planes: not started. Doing
  this now, before occupancy is solved, would mean guessing at full rectangles
  - exactly the "rectangular extent is not proof of occupancy" mistake M4
  exists to avoid.
- Occlusion-edge and reprojection-based joint fitting (the milestone's other
  named mechanisms): not started, genuinely open, comparable in scope to the
  per-cell occupancy problem already spent on.

## Decision this hands to Astra

Whether M4 continues as production design with length, plane-identity, and
quadrant as **required user inputs** (consistent with the standing camera
manifest principle: production has to do without things the fixtures provide
for free, but it does not follow that production has zero inputs at all - a
person confirming "this is the same wall" is a very different ask than an F3
overlay), and whether per-cell occupancy is worth one more specific,
differently-shaped attempt (occlusion-edge/silhouette detection at plane
boundaries was the one idea I had left unnamed) or is better left unsolved for
now with holes/uncertainty reported honestly rather than guessed at.
